Roster & Salary Cap

DraftKings Classic CFB: QB, RB, RB, WR/TE, WR/TE, WR/TE, FLEX (RB/WR/TE) — 7 players, $50,000 cap. FanDuel Classic CFB: QB, RB, RB, WR, WR, WR, FLEX — 7 players, $60,000 cap; TEs fill the WR slots (no separate TE slot) and the FLEX is a Super Flex, so a second QB is fair game there too — and since a QB is normally the highest-projecting body available for that slot, the generator plays one by default (uncheck “Play a 2nd QB in the Super Flex” to let any position win it). Use the site toggle above to switch — both are best-known-standard assumptions, verify against your actual contest's rules page since either site can vary slot structure by slate.

Value & Roster Balance

Spend up on 1–2 "core" plays you're confident in, then fill out the roster with salary-efficient value (points per $1,000) rather than a lineup of eight mid-tier plays. A $4,000 RB projected for 12 points is often a better roster fit than a $6,500 RB projected for 15.

Stacking (Correlation)

A QB's big game and his top target's big game tend to happen together — pairing them means one player's ceiling outcome lifts your other player too, instead of your roster's outcomes being independent coin flips. QB + a pass-catcher from the same team is the single most common CFB DFS building block.

The generator treats that as the only legal same-team pairing: one QB plus exactly one of his own WR/TE. It will not build RB+WR, WR+WR, WR+TE, RB+RB, or a QB with two pass-catchers. Those share a team without sharing the upside — two receivers compete for one finite target pool, and a back and a receiver split the same drives, so the game that makes one hit is usually the game that leaves the other short. You get the downside of concentration with none of the correlation.

Game Stacks & Bring-Backs

In a high-total, closely-spread game (a projected shootout), pieces from both sidelines can pay off — if you stack a QB and WR from one team, a "bring-back" piece from the opponent (their own QB or lead receiver) hedges against your original team simply losing the shootout.

Game Script

Big favorites tend to run more once ahead (RB volume climbs); big underdogs tend to throw more chasing points (WR/TE volume climbs). This is exactly what this project's own pace/script model already adjusts for — the same signal driving the Props and Impact pages' "this week" projections feeds these DK numbers too.

GPP vs. Cash Leverage

Large multi-entry tournaments (guaranteed prize pools, top-heavy payouts like the contest in your screenshot) reward differentiation — a correlated, lower-owned stack that hits big beats a lineup of obvious chalk when only the top few finishes get paid. Head-to-head / 50-50 games reward the opposite: safer, higher-floor plays with less variance.
